Somewhere past the second or third lot, most banks tap out.

Some lenders will write three or four townhouses under a standard residential policy, no questions asked.

Others stop counting at two, and anything past that gets pushed straight into commercial credit, with a different set of questions.

A few will go to five, then ask for pre-sales the moment a sixth lot is added to the plan.

The line is real. It is just drawn in a different place at every lender, and the expensive way to find yours is to apply and wait for the answer.

Knowing where each lender's line sits, before the file goes in, is the job.

Waiting is a line on the feasibility. It just never gets written down.

  • Holding costs
  • Builder's quote, expiring
  • Construction costs, rising
  • Margin, shrinking

Finance delay is a cost that never appears as a line item, and it always comes out of the margin.

Here for the best rate? Ask about these first.

  2. How much leverage they'll provide
  3. What pre-sales they require

  5. Then rate

Rate is real money, and it is still the wrong first question, because the sharpest rate in the market funds nothing if the first four are wrong.

Six townhouses, DA approved. Here's what credit sees.

  • 1 Equity & leverage

    How much of the project cost you're funding yourself, and how much of the site's value the lender can rely on.

  • 2 Pre-sale cover

    Whether contracted sales cover enough of the debt to make the construction facility serviceable.

  • 3 Builder & delivery

    Whether the builder and the building contract can actually deliver the project on time and on budget.

  • 4 Exit

    How the debt gets repaid, whether that's sale, refinance, or hold.

A good project can still get the wrong answer when the file doesn't tell this story clearly.
